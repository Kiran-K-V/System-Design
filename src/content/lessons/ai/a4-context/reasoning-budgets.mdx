## The problem

A support tool has two kinds of request. Most are lookups: "What is the return window?" A few are multi-step. Example: "Order 4471 shipped in two parcels, one was damaged, the coupon was applied to the wrong parcel. What do we refund?" The team switches the model to thinking mode to fix the hard cases. The easy cases now take far longer and cost several times more.

A **reasoning model** spends tokens working on a problem before it answers. Those **thinking tokens** (also called reasoning tokens) are generated like any other output, one token at a time. The idea is **test-time compute**. Instead of only making the model bigger or training it longer ([the Scaling Laws and Model Choice lesson](/learn/ai/a1-model-function/scaling-laws/)), you let it spend more compute. The model spends the extra compute on each hard question, at the moment of the question. The cost is paid per request, so it has a latency price and a money price. This lesson shows both, and how to cap them.

## How thinking changes a request

The [Sampling and Decoding](/learn/ai/a1-model-function/sampling-decoding/) lesson ([sampling and decoding](/learn/ai/a1-model-function/sampling-decoding/)) showed that a model produces one token at a time. Each token costs about the same time. Thinking does not change that. It adds tokens before the answer. Two things follow.

**Latency.** The answer cannot start until the thinking ends. Thousands of thinking tokens at normal decode speed mean tens of seconds of silence. Real speed varies by model and load, so measure yours. The first answer token comes after that wait. Streaming does not help, because the wait is before the stream. This is a time-to-first-token (TTFT) cost. Processing the prompt adds its own wait before any token appears. This lesson counts only the thinking part.

**Cost.** Thinking tokens bill as output tokens. Anthropic's docs say you pay for the full thinking, even when the API returns only a short summary of it. OpenAI says reasoning tokens are not visible through the API. They "still occupy space in the model's context window and are billed as" output tokens. Check the output price on the [Anthropic pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing). Thinking can be many times longer than the answer, so it often dominates the bill for the call. Watch the timeline.

## Controlling thinking today

Providers have changed how you control thinking. The rules below are what the docs say as of 2026-10-16. They change often. Check the page for your model before you ship, and keep the idea, not the parameter name.

**Anthropic.** Two modes exist.

- *Manual budget:* `thinking: {type: "enabled", budget_tokens: N}`. The budget must be at least 1,024 and less than `max_tokens`. It is "a target rather than a strict cap": the model may stop sooner, and `max_tokens` is the hard ceiling. The docs suggest starting near 1,024 for simple tasks and at 16,000 or more for complex ones. They also suggest using batch processing above 32,000 tokens. This mode is deprecated on the Claude 4.6 models, which still accept it. Claude 4.7 and later models reject it with a 400 error. The docs name Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1, and Haiku 5.5 among them. On Claude 4.5 and earlier thinking models, it is the only mode.
- *Adaptive thinking with effort:* `thinking: {type: "adaptive"}` plus `output_config: {effort: ...}`. The model decides per request whether to think and how much. The levels are `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, and `max`. `high` is the default on most models. `medium` is the default on Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5. Effort is "soft guidance", not a token count, and which levels exist varies by model. On Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, lowering the effort works better than asking in the prompt for less thinking.

In both modes thinking counts toward `max_tokens`. If `stop_reason` is `max_tokens`, raise the cap or lower the effort. The response field `usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens` reports how many billed output tokens were thinking. A change to the effort or the budget between requests invalidates the cached message blocks (the [Cache-friendly Prompt Layout](/learn/ai/a4-context/cache-friendly-layout/) lesson). So pick one setting per conversation.

For **OpenAI**, the `reasoning` effort takes values from `none`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, and `max`. Which values are valid depends on the model. The guide says GPT-6 Astra rejects `none` with an HTTP 400. GPT-6.1 Sol rejects `none` and `minimal`, and it defaults to `medium`. Reasoning may range "from a few hundred to tens of thousands" of tokens. OpenAI recommends reserving at least 25,000 tokens for reasoning and output when you start. The reason is that the output limit includes the reasoning. You can request a reasoning summary with the `summary` parameter. With function calling, it recommends passing back the reasoning items returned with the last call.

The common shape: a soft control (effort) and a hard cap (`max_tokens`). Set the cap high enough for thinking plus the answer.

## Where reasoning comes from

DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) shows that this behavior can be trained with reinforcement learning (RL) on checkable rewards. The reward is rule-based. It has an accuracy part. A math answer in a set format can be checked by a program, and code can be tested. It also has a format part: put the reasoning between think tags. The R1-Zero version was trained with RL alone. It raised its average pass@1 score on the AIME 2024 math contest from 15.6% to 77.9% during training. Its thinking time grew steadily. The final DeepSeek-R1 scores 79.8% on AIME 2024 and 97.3% on MATH-500. Those are single-benchmark numbers from the paper's own evaluation. The evaluation used tasks with checkable answers, which is why RL works there. They do not predict gains on open-ended tasks.

## Pick a budget

How much thinking is enough? It depends on the task, and the gain flattens: Anthropic says returns diminish "depend on the task". The explorer shows how latency and cost grow with the budget. Its accuracy curve is **made up**, to show a knee where extra tokens stop paying. No paper we read measures accuracy against thinking tokens for a current model. Run this test on your own set: score 50 or more real tasks at 3 or 4 settings. Then pick the cheapest setting that reaches your quality bar.

What to notice:

1. **Thinking dominates the wait and the bill.** Most of the time and cost of the call is spent before the first answer token.
2. **Model price matters.** Switch between a cheap and an expensive model and the same call changes cost by a large factor.
3. **Latency grows in a straight line.** Every extra thousand thinking tokens adds the same extra wait. A UI with a tight limit leaves almost no room for thinking.
4. **The knee is a property of your task.** On the made-up curve it sits at one point. Yours may be much lower or much higher.

## When to use reasoning

| Task | Use thinking? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Extract fields from an invoice | No | One pass reads the answer. Thinking adds latency and cost for no gain. |
| Classify a short message | No, or `low` effort | The label is a direct pattern. |
| Multi-step math, planning, hard code | Yes | Intermediate steps are the work. |
| Multi-step tool use (agents) | Yes, on planning steps | Models can think between tool calls. Most reasoning happens on the first request after new input. |
| A chat with a 3-second latency budget | Usually no | No thinking budget fits. |
| A background job or batch | Yes, with a high cap | Nobody waits. |

State the latency budget first. A tight budget leaves room for very few tokens, and those tokens hold both thinking and the answer. Then choose the model and effort that fit it. A common design routes easy requests to a cheap model or low effort. The design gives thinking to the requests a classifier flags as multi-step. Reasoning also reduces some errors, but it does not remove hallucination (the [Why Models Hallucinate, and the Context Window](/learn/ai/a1-model-function/hallucination-context/) lesson, [hallucination and context](/learn/ai/a1-model-function/hallucination-context/)). A model can reason its way to a confident wrong answer, so check outputs where it matters.

## Trade-offs

| Choice | You gain | You pay | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| No thinking | Lowest latency and cost | Weak on multi-step problems | Lookups, extraction, short answers |
| Low effort | Some reasoning at small cost | Smaller gains on hard tasks | Mixed traffic with tight latency |
| High effort or large budget | Better on hard tasks | Latency in tens of seconds, higher bill | Hard problems, offline work |
| Fixed per-conversation setting | The prompt cache keeps working | Cannot tune per turn | Long conversations with a cached prefix |
| Routing by difficulty | Pay for thinking only when needed | A classifier that can be wrong | Mixed traffic at volume |

## Interview lens

<Callout type="tip">
Say it in words: "Thinking tokens come before the answer and bill as output. So I route: simple requests skip thinking, and hard ones get a capped budget. I run an eval to find the knee." Mention that thinking tokens bill even when the thinking is hidden.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
Do not turn thinking on for every request "for quality". Do not treat `budget_tokens` as a hard cap: it is a target, and `max_tokens` is the limit. Do not hard-code one provider's parameter, since the rules differ by model and change. Say "a soft effort control and a hard token cap".
</Callout>
